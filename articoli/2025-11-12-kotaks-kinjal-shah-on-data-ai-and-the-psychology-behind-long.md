# Kotak’s Kinjal Shah on data, AI and the psychology behind long-term investing

# Kotak’s Kinjal Shah on data, AI and the psychology behind long-term investing

                    In conversation with ET Brand Equity, the head of Marketing, Digital Business & Analytics, Kotak Mahindra AMC, shares why the brand decided to make “quick-money addiction” its theme for the mass campaign
                                    

            
        
                    
                    
                    
                                        Kotak Mutual Fund’s recent campaign on “quick-money addiction” steps away from the industry’s usual product-first tone to address a behavioural blind spot among investors. The idea, Kinjal Shah, Head of Marketing, Digital Business & Analytics, Kotak Mahindra AMC, says, was born from ground-level conversations that revealed a familiar pattern — “an impatience” for returns and a fascination with faster, riskier routes such as F&O trading, online gaming, and lotteries.


For Shah, the challenge lay in framing this insight without sounding preachy or opportunistic. At the same time, it was also about changing the narrative at the last moment, as the release of the campaign coincided with the ‘Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act, 2025’, which banned online real-money games in India. The campaign, positioned as a long-term habit-change initiative, seeks to make investors pause and reflect rather than promise instant gains.


In this conversation, Shah discusses how data-driven segmentation, AI adoption, and faster data warehousing are helping the brand interpret customer intent more precisely. He also outlines how Kotak Mutual Fund is reshaping its communication model, from mass awareness to personalised nudges — to keep pace with a rapidly evolving investor mindset.



It had nothing to do with Diwali. Throughout the year, when we speak to people, we try to understand what they are doing, what their habits are, and how they behave. When we engaged with them, we learned that the idea of quick money is present in everyone’s mind. Everyone wants to make quick money. We then explored the routes people choose to pursue it. Many feel that SIPs are boring and take too long to build wealth, so they believe they won’t make money that way. Then, alternative routes emerged quite starkly during our conversations. People mentioned investing in F&O (Futures and Options) to make quick money, engaging in online gaming, and even considering lotteries, thinking they could earn a lot quickly.


These insights became our starting point, which led us to internal discussions about how to approach this campaign.



The core measurement for a campaign like this is whether we can reach the right audience. Maximising reach becomes the primary KPI, ensuring that the message is delivered to the appropriate target group at scale. Since this is an investor education campaign, traditional KPIs such as direct business impact do not apply.


The key metrics are reach and engagement. If people are engaging with the content, it indicates that the objective is being met. Strong view-through rates, video completion, and interaction with the webpage help us understand how the audience is responding.


On television, we rely on BARC to measure campaign performance. On digital platforms, we identify audience segments relevant to our target group and run ads accordingly. Most platforms, such as Google and Meta, provide cohort-level data — enough to reach audiences who are actively engaging in related activities, such as gaming or financial content.




Take the lottery, for example. We consider them as a cohort based on their behaviour on search. The same goes for online gaming, and the same goes for F&O trading or any other trading for that matter. The primary objective is F&O trading, where people are actually missing out.


So that’s what we try to figure out — what is their search behaviour? Epigraphy is finding out what kinds of apps are installed across all platforms. Based on those signals, you are able to reach out to consumers.




This is a habit-changing campaign. It is not going to happen overnight. If someone is addicted to something, I cannot just show up once and say it is not a good habit; it does not change immediately. Habit change takes time. For people to believe that a systematic way of investing can help build their fortune, it will take time.




Typically, the internal team first debates what route we need to take. Making a loud statement like “it’s an addiction” is not something people will easily accept. Even youngsters or millennials would not be in a zone of acceptance. So it was important to define that it is, in a way, an addiction — that everyone wants to make quick money. In this process, people end up losing their entire fortune. That insight came from internal discussions.


This challenge was then given to the agency, with the belief that this route could change the game. It had to follow a kind of de-addiction approach. Then the communication could touch the heart, making people realise that they too are addicted and need to move out of it.


That is the first step. Then we involve multiple agencies, give them the same brief, and evaluate who comes closest to our expectations.




We have, but we are still open to pitches because creativity is not associated with one person. There are so many creative and brilliant minds in India, so why not explore that? Thus, we open up the gates for large campaigns.




Betting exists in many forms. Certain formats may have been banned, but others still continue. We did debate this internally, especially since it happened just a few days before we went live. That’s why we consciously decided to go ahead and target the mindset or behaviour around quick money.


We had to make a few changes and tweak our stories — especially the words. It had to be done because the government had already taken steps in that direction. It would have been fortunate if it had gone live at the same time, but we just tweaked it slightly to ensure it would still attract attention and maintain relatability.




When you enter as a customer in an AMC, we create a footprint — whether you are investing directly, via an RIA (Registered Investment Advisor), or through a distributor. All of this is available through your transaction history with us. Based on that, we keep building your history.


Apart from that, there are multiple touchpoints. You might be happy or unhappy; you may want services, and you might reach out to us, CAMS, or various platforms for servicing. We have to ensure that any communication happening there also gets recorded. That is how the entire single customer view is created. Based on this profile, we create various cohorts and maintain that record.




It is cohort-driven only. One-to-many communication has to be cohort-driven. A lot of communication could be one-to-many; it could be a blast. And there will be many communications that are specific to you. There could be service emailers applicable only to you and not to me.


I have to ensure that your SIPs are serviced on time. If you have done transactions, all of that comes in — those are personalised communications. Then comes cohort-based communication, showing what people like you are doing with us. That may help create the right nudge. Many investors still don’t know where to invest or how much to invest. Small nudges help them understand.




AI is already changing how the entire stack functions. Communications are already being driven by AI. It helps reduce the load of creating communication. If you want to layer agentic on top of it, that is the next step and is already in place. Most are working on it to increase agent capacity in call centres. Ultimately, how many humans can I recruit after a point? If you want scale and personalised service, agentic is the next step.


If today a human agent makes 25 to 50 connects in an hour, that can go up to 1,000 or 2,000 with agentic. You just configure it, and the scale becomes unlimited. If we have seven or eight million customers and want to reach them within a month, it is possible — something human capacity cannot achieve. Agentic AI will play a major role in that.


The second big advantage is standardisation. If we are three human agents, we may interpret things very differently, even with a script. But agentic AI, driven by bots, will also play a role in product communication.




That’s where agentic AI comes into the picture. It’s not a voice bot or a voice blast. Agentic AI will also interact with my database and the customer’s single view. Based on the footprint we have understood about you, all configurations will be automatically set. It is as good as me talking to you. It will dynamically understand what you are saying and, based on that, retrieve data in real time and continue the conversation.




That’s where the data warehouse comes into the picture. If I run AI on traditional data, it could take 20 days. I’m giving you an example: before we implemented the warehouse, running cohorts on our full data used to take 20 days. Now it takes two to three hours. That’s the difference. So your data warehouse needs to be ready.


People are shifting. It’s not rocket science. Everyone understands the challenges and is slowly building these warehouses. That’s what makes them AI-ready. AI is no longer a choice; it’s about how fast you adapt and how correctly. You should not fall into the FOMO of AI. That’s another problem. It should not be a tick-box exercise — doing five things on Perplexity or OpenAI and claiming you’re using AI. At the enterprise level, many things come into play. You may not want everything AI offers. AI must have strong data security layers. That’s something you need to be ready for.




There are definitely vendors or solution providers who are very mature in this space. It is equally our responsibility to identify the right solution partners. You cannot build every talent in-house, so you have to rely on partners. That’s where sourcing the right partner becomes important; they are mature enough and, in turn, help us mature.


To become AI-ready, you have to ensure the data is right. That starts with converting unstructured data from legacy systems into structured data. On top of that, you apply the AI layer. The real task begins with legacy systems and unstructured data, moving to structured data layers. Then, ultimately, you convert it to vectors and let the agents operate independently.
